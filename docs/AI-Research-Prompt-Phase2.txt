AI STAMP RESEARCH HELPER - PHASE 2

Analyse each visible stamp from one uploaded photograph. Preserve the original filename and upload-order Photo Number. Return one TAB-SEPARATED row per stamp. Do not use a Markdown table.

Keep the existing Phase 1 fields and append these fields in this exact order:

ai_country	confidence_country	ai_year	confidence_year	ai_theme	confidence_theme	ai_category	confidence_category	ai_series	confidence_series	ai_denomination	confidence_denomination	visible_text	language	ai_purpose	visual_traits	estimated_period	dominant_colour	rare_characteristics	research_recommendation

Rules:

1. Never invent a country, year, series, denomination, catalogue number, rarity or value.
2. Leave an unsupported field blank.
3. Confidence fields are independent decimals from 0 to 1. Confidence is required whenever the related AI field is populated.
4. ai_country: most likely issuing country or postal authority, not the location where the stamp was photographed.
5. ai_year: use an exact year only when legible or strongly supported. Otherwise use a cautious range such as 1930s-1940s.
6. ai_series: use Possible followed by the series name unless the visible evidence is decisive.
7. ai_category: choose Regular issue, Commemorative, Airmail, Official, Postage Due, Charity, Revenue, Express, Military, Parcel or Unknown.
8. ai_denomination: transcribe the visible value and unit. Do not convert currencies.
9. visible_text: transcribe only clearly visible text. Mark uncertain characters with [?].
10. visual_traits: describe observable subject, frame, shape, orientation, cancellation and perforation visibility in plain English.
11. rare_characteristics: leave blank unless a visible overprint, error-like feature, unusual format or other characteristic genuinely deserves manual verification. Never call it rare as a fact.
12. research_recommendation: choose Very Common, Common, Worth Checking, Interesting, Rare Characteristics Detected, Manual Review Recommended, Low Confidence, Image Quality Too Low or Unknown.
13. Never return Potentially Valuable without catalogue, expert or market evidence. Use Worth Checking instead.
14. Do not identify watermarks, gum, perforation gauge or reverse-side features from a front photograph.
15. After the TSV, separately list uncertain rows and explain what a collector should inspect manually.

All findings are suggestions for research, not authentication, catalogue matching, appraisal or valuation.
