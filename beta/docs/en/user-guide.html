AI Stamp Collection Scanner

User Guide — Browser Beta v1.0

A practical guide to photos, automatic detection, inventory review and saving your work.

1. Purpose

The Browser Beta helps find visible stamps in your photos and places them in an editable, photo-linked stamp inventory. User review remains important.

2. Add photos

JPG/JPEG, PNG and WebP are supported. Each upload receives its own photo number, even if filenames are the same. One inventory can contain up to 250 photos.

✓ Good

Straight from above, in focus, with enough light. A little overlap is fine — leave at least half of every stamp visible.

△ Difficult but usable

A stamp about half hidden under a neighbour still works, as long as its country name or face value stays readable. Check those rows extra carefully.

✕ Not recommended

Stamps buried under others, with the country name and the value hidden. Slide them apart and photograph again.

Rule of thumb: at least 50% of each stamp visible, and either the country name or the face value readable. This is an inventory tool, not a valuation — the photo only has to be good enough to find and identify a stamp, not to judge its condition.

Why overlap is difficult: when part of a stamp is hidden under another stamp, its complete shape is not present in the photo. The scanner may miss it or treat two stamps as one object.

3. Create the inventory

Open the AI Stamp Scanner, select your photos and choose Create inventory. The detector tries to find each visible physical stamp separately and creates inventory records for detected stamps.

AI identification (optional)

Want country, period, value and more filled in automatically instead of "Unknown"? Paste your own free Google Gemini API key at the top of the scanner page (get one at Google AI Studio, no credit card required). The key stays only in your own browser. Rows filled in this way get the status "AI suggestion" and, like every automatic result, still need review.

4. Multiple and identical stamps

Each separately visible physical stamp should produce a separate inventory record, including visually identical stamps. Quantity is an editable collector field, not a substitute for detecting individual objects. Always compare the record count with the photo.

5. Review and edit

Compare the inventory with the source photo. Pay extra attention to crowded album pages, rotated stamps, dark photos and overlapping stamps. Add or correct unknown details where needed.

6. Local storage, privacy and analytics

Stamp photos and draft inventory data are processed locally in your browser and are not uploaded for stamp identification. Anonymous usage analytics may be collected to improve the beta. Browser storage can be cleared, so save completed work regularly as Excel or CSV.

7. Save your inventory

You can save the inventory as an Excel or CSV file. Both contain Record ID, Photo Number, Original Filename and Image Reference. Photos and thumbnails are not embedded in Excel in Browser Beta v1.0.

Browser Beta assists with detection and inventory creation, but automatic results should always be reviewed.

Limit: detection does not mean every stamp will certainly be found or identified. Browser Beta v1.0 does not determine value, rarity, authenticity, watermark, exact perforation or catalogue number.
